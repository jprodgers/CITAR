# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Criticise the work, not the person.

## What that means here

**Welcome**

- Questions that seem obvious. Everybody was new once, and CITAR spans game rules, language models
  and server operations — nobody knows all three.
- Disagreement about technical decisions, argued on the merits.
- "This documentation is wrong and here is why."
- Saying you do not know something.

**Not welcome**

- Personal attacks, insults, or making someone the subject rather than their argument.
- Harassment of any kind, in public or private, including unwanted contact after being asked to
  stop.
- Comments about someone's identity — who they are, where they are from, what they believe, how
  they speak or write.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled decisions to make a point, or derailing threads.

This applies in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and any other project space,
and to behaviour elsewhere that affects someone's ability to take part here.

## A note on tone

Technical disagreement is not rudeness, and politeness is not agreement. "This approach will not
work, because X" is fine. "This is idiotic" is not — it says nothing about X and makes the
conversation about the person instead.

English is not everyone's first language. Read generously before deciding something was meant
badly.

## Reporting

If something here is not right, tell the maintainer privately through
[a GitHub private message](https://github.com/jprodgers) or by opening a
[private security advisory](https://github.com/jprodgers/CITAR/security/advisories/new), which is
private and does not require an actual vulnerability.

Reports are read by one person. What you can expect: an acknowledgement, a conversation about what
happened, and a decision. You will not be identified to the person you reported without your
agreement.

## What happens

Depending on what happened and whether it was repeated: a private word, a public correction, having
a comment edited or removed, a temporary block, or a permanent ban.

The maintainer makes these decisions and is bound by the same rules. A maintainer who behaves badly
is a worse problem than a contributor who does, not a smaller one.
